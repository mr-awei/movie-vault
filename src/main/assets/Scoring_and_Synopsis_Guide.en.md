# General Film Rating & Synopsis Standard
### Version: v4.0
### Purpose: Unified Film Classification, Rating, Synopsis, and Metadata Standard for the “Yinghai Film List” (Excel)

---

# 1. Purpose of This Standard

This standard is designed to unify the following aspects of the “Yinghai Film List”:

- Film classification
- Recommendation ratings
- Plot synopses
- Metadata and tags
- Excel data structure

It applies to mainstream films, arthouse films, genre films, B-movies, erotic films, horror films, crime films, cult films, low-budget productions, exploitation films, and film series.

The primary goal is not to make descriptions sound sophisticated, but to make film information:

> **Accurate, understandable, comparable, and useful for long-term collection management.**

The two main components have different purposes:

> **The rating answers: “How well is this film made, and how worthwhile is it within its category?”**

> **The synopsis answers: “What is this film actually about?”**

These two functions must not be confused.

---

# 2. Film Rating System

## 2.1 Basic Rules

Use a **10-point rating scale**, with the final score rounded to **2 decimal places**.

Ratings should reflect the film’s actual quality and its position within its genre rather than being determined simply by popularity, box office performance, or ratings from external platforms.

For low-budget erotic films, Category III films, B-movies, exploitation films, cult films, and similar works:

> **The primary comparison should be with films of similar genre, era, production scale, and intended audience.**

A film should not automatically receive a low score simply because it is not a mainstream blockbuster.

Likewise, controversial, erotic, violent, or exploitative subject matter should not automatically increase its score.

---

# 3. Ten Rating Dimensions

The final rating consists of the following ten dimensions:

| Rating Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Screenplay / Writing | 15% |
| Direction & Staging | 14% |
| Acting | 13% |
| Cinematography & Production Design | 12% |
| Editing & Pacing | 10% |
| Music & Sound | 9% |
| Themes & Intellectual Depth | 8% |
| Originality & Distinctiveness | 7% |
| Production Quality & Technical Completion | 6% |
| Series Positioning / Genre Significance & Reputation Continuity | 6% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

Calculation:

> **Final Rating = Weighted sum of all ten dimension scores**

Each dimension should generally be evaluated on a 10-point scale before applying its corresponding weight.

The final result must be presented with two decimal places.

---

# 4. Detailed Rating Dimensions

## 4.1 Screenplay / Writing — 15%

Evaluate:

- Story completeness
- Narrative logic
- Character motivation
- Conflict
- Plot progression
- Dialogue
- Character consistency
- Quality of adaptation, when applicable
- Obvious logical flaws

The key question is:

> **“How well does the story itself work?”**

For erotic films, horror films, crime films, B-movies, and other genre films, the story should be evaluated according to their intended genre and scale.

A simple story does not automatically deserve a low score.

---

## 4.2 Direction & Staging — 14%

Evaluate the director’s control over the overall presentation:

- Scene staging
- Camera direction
- Actor blocking
- Atmosphere
- Genre execution
- Narrative control
- Scene organization
- Directorial style

The key question is:

> **“Has the director effectively translated the story into a film?”**

---

## 4.3 Acting — 13%

Evaluate the overall performance of:

- Main actors
- Supporting actors
- Character portrayal
- Emotional expression
- Character credibility
- Dialogue delivery
- Chemistry between actors
- Whether performances frequently break immersion

Non-professional actors and low-budget productions should be judged according to their own production context rather than automatically against major Hollywood-level performances.

---

## 4.4 Cinematography & Production Design — 12%

Evaluate:

- Cinematography
- Composition
- Lighting
- Color
- Sets
- Costumes
- Makeup
- Props
- Overall visual style

The key consideration is whether the film creates a visual language that fits its story and genre.

---

## 4.5 Editing & Pacing — 10%

Evaluate:

- Editing flow
- Narrative progression
- Pacing
- Dragging or unnecessary slow sections
- Excessive jumps
- Repetition
- Effectiveness of pacing in action, horror, erotic, and other genre scenes

---

## 4.6 Music & Sound — 9%

Evaluate:

- Suitability of the score
- Sound effects
- Sound design
- Atmosphere enhancement
- Whether music overwhelms the film
- Effectiveness of sound in horror, suspense, action, and other key scenes

---

## 4.7 Themes & Intellectual Depth — 8%

Evaluate whether the film contains:

- A clear theme
- Character-level ideas
- Social significance
- Exploration of human nature
- Genre-level commentary
- Reflection of real-world issues

Important:

> **A film does not need a profound theme to be good.**

A purely entertainment-oriented genre film can still receive a high rating through strong writing, direction, acting, pacing, and execution.

Do not artificially assign philosophical or social significance that is not actually present in the film.

---

## 4.8 Originality & Distinctiveness — 7%

Evaluate:

- Originality of the story
- Unique concepts
- Effective genre combinations
- Distinctive style
- Differences from similar works

Older films should be evaluated in the context of their time.

Do not judge historical originality entirely by modern standards after certain ideas have already become common.

---

## 4.9 Production Quality & Technical Completion — 6%

Evaluate:

- Production scale
- Set quality
- Cinematography technology
- Special effects
- Post-production
- Costumes and makeup
- Sound
- Overall technical completion

Important:

> **Low budget ≠ low quality.**

A low-budget film can still score well if it uses its limited resources effectively and accomplishes what it sets out to do.

---

## 4.10 Series Positioning / Genre Significance & Reputation Continuity — 6%

For films that belong to a series, evaluate:

- Position within the series
- Whether it fulfills its intended role
- Continuity of series characteristics
- Improvement or decline compared with related entries
- Whether it became a representative work of its genre
- Influence within its genre

For standalone films:

> **Evaluate their significance and representativeness within their respective genre.**

---

# 5. Rating Bands

| Score | Rating |
|---|---|
| 9.20–10.00 | Hall of Fame / Masterpiece |
| 8.50–9.15 | Masterpiece |
| 7.80–8.45 | Excellent |
| 7.00–7.75 | Good |
| 6.30–6.95 | Passing |
| 5.50–6.25 | Mediocre |
| 4.50–5.45 | Below Average |
| 3.00–4.45 | Poor |
| 0–2.95 | Extremely Poor |

Ratings must maintain consistency and reasonable distribution.

Do not artificially push scores upward to make a film list look better.

Do not systematically suppress scores merely to appear strict.

---

# 6. Genre-Specific Rating Principles

## 6.1 Erotic Films / Category III / Low-Budget Erotic Films

These films must primarily be compared with works of similar genre.

Typical reference range:

> **5.80–6.80**

Exceptional works may exceed this range.

The erotic nature of a film:

- Does not automatically add points
- Does not automatically subtract points

Erotic content should only affect the score when it has an impact on:

- Writing
- Acting
- Direction
- Pacing
- Genre execution

### Genre Reference Point

**“Sex and Zen: The Scroll of Ecstasy” / “玉蒲团之偷情宝鉴”: 6.7+**

This serves as a relatively high reference point for erotic / Category III films.

---

## 6.2 Crime / Exploitation / Extreme Genre Films

Focus on:

- Crime narrative
- Suspense
- Characterization
- Genre execution
- Atmosphere
- Social realism or contextual relevance

Violence, gore, erotic content, or disturbing subject matter should not automatically reduce the rating.

### Genre Reference Point

**“The Untold Story” / “八仙饭店之人肉叉烧包”: 7.5+**

This serves as a relatively high reference point for crime / exploitation / extreme genre films.

---

# 7. Rating Discipline

## 7.1 Do Not Copy Platform Ratings

Ratings from:

- Douban
- IMDb
- Rotten Tomatoes
- Letterboxd
- Maoyan
- Other platforms

may be consulted as references, but:

> **They must not simply be copied into this system.**

---

## 7.2 Do Not Add Points for Fame

A famous director, famous actor, or historically famous film does not automatically receive extra points.

---

## 7.3 Do Not Add Points for Controversial Subject Matter

Erotic, violent, bloody, taboo, or controversial subject matter does not constitute a reason for a higher score by itself.

---

## 7.4 Do Not Automatically Penalize Low Production Budgets

A film must be judged according to how effectively it performs within its own production conditions.

---

## 7.5 Maintain Consistency Within Each Batch

When rating multiple films together:

> **All films in the same batch must be evaluated using the same rating scale and standards.**

The rating standard must not change depending on when a film is processed.

# 8. Film Synopsis — Core Standard

## 8.1 Primary Purpose of the Synopsis

The single most important purpose of the synopsis is:

> **A person who has never seen the film should understand what the film is actually about after reading the synopsis.**

The synopsis is NOT:

- A film review
- A recommendation
- Promotional copy
- A collection of genre labels
- A thematic analysis
- An artistic critique

It should first and foremost be:

> **A concise, accurate, readable summary of the actual story.**

---

# 9. Six Questions the Synopsis Should Answer

A good synopsis should, whenever possible, answer:

### 1. Who is the protagonist?

Explain the main character’s identity, occupation, relationship, or situation.

### 2. What is happening at the beginning?

Establish the starting situation.

### 3. What event changes the situation?

Explain the inciting incident or major event.

### 4. What conflict does the protagonist face?

Clearly identify the central problem or conflict.

### 5. How does the story develop?

Briefly explain the major progression of the plot.

### 6. What is the film broadly about?

A final sentence may briefly mention the film’s genre characteristics or central theme.

---

# 10. Recommended Synopsis Structure

Use the following general structure:

> **Character / Setting → Inciting Event → Central Conflict → Plot Development → Genre / Distinctive Features**

Example structure:

> A certain character is a ______ who comes to ______ because of ______. An event involving ______ causes them to become involved in ______. After discovering ______, they are forced to ______. As the situation develops, ______ happens, causing the conflict between ______ and ______ to escalate. The film primarily focuses on ______, with additional elements of ______.

This is a structural guideline only.

> **Do not mechanically reuse the same sentence patterns for every film.**

---

# 11. Plot Information Takes Priority

Recommended content ratio:

> **Plot information: 60–80%**

> **Film characteristics / themes / atmosphere: 20–40%**

If a film has a complicated story, the synopsis should devote even more space to explaining the plot.

Do not remove important plot information merely to add:

- Themes
- Artistic style
- Cinematography
- Intellectual interpretation

---

# 12. Avoid Vague Synopses

The following expressions should NOT be used as substitutes for actual plot information:

- “The film revolves around...”
- “The story follows...”
- “The film explores...”
- “The film depicts...”
- “The film examines...”
- “The film reveals...”
- “Through..., the film portrays...”
- “It offers a deep exploration of human nature...”
- “It creates a strong sense of...”
- “It is filled with...”

These phrases may occasionally be used in the final sentence.

However:

> **They must not replace concrete descriptions of what actually happens.**

### ❌ Poor Example

> The film revolves around complicated relationships between men and women, exploring human desire while combining erotic, suspenseful, and horror elements.

Problem:

> The reader still does not know who the characters are or what actually happens.

### ✅ Good Example

> A certain character meets another person after ______, and the two become involved in ______. When ______ happens, the protagonist discovers ______ and is forced to ______. As the investigation develops, the situation becomes connected to ______, causing their relationship to change. The film combines ______ with elements of ______.

The second version provides actual story information.

---

# 13. Spoiler Policy

The synopsis:

> **May reveal the premise and major plot developments, but should avoid directly revealing the ending.**

It is acceptable to reveal:

- Main characters
- Setting
- Inciting event
- Central case
- Character relationships
- Main conflict
- Major developments in the first and middle parts
- How the central conflict escalates

Try to avoid revealing:

- Final ending
- Final killer identity
- Final villain identity
- Major final twists
- Whether the protagonist ultimately survives
- Final truth or revelation

Unless such information is absolutely necessary to explain the basic story.

The principle is:

> **Tell the reader what happens, but do not tell them the final outcome.**

---

# 14. Synopsis Length

Recommended Chinese synopsis length:

> **100–180 Chinese characters**

For English-language versions, use an equivalent concise length appropriate to the amount of plot information.

A synopsis may be slightly shorter when the story is extremely simple.

However:

> **Do not sacrifice essential plot information merely to make the synopsis shorter.**

Do not add empty thematic or promotional language simply to reach a target word count.

---

# 15. Synopsis Accuracy Requirements

This is a key requirement of v4.0.

Before writing a synopsis, verify:

- Official title
- Release year
- Director
- Main characters
- Story setting
- Inciting event
- Core plot
- Basic narrative development

Preferred sources include:

- Official film information
- Reliable film databases
- Major film reference sources
- Film synopsis / plot summaries
- Multiple independent sources for cross-checking

Douban, Baidu Baike, IMDb, Wikipedia, and similar sources may be used as references where appropriate.

However:

> **Do not mechanically copy source text.**

---

# 16. Never Guess the Plot from the Title

If you only know a title such as:

> “A Certain Erotic Tale”

> “A Certain Strange Case”

> “A Certain Tragic Case”

you must not infer the plot from the title alone.

If information is insufficient:

> **Research the film first, then write the synopsis.**

If the plot still cannot be confirmed:

> **It is better to write a shorter synopsis than to invent characters, crimes, events, twists, or endings.**

---

# 17. Synopsis Priorities by Genre

## Erotic Films

Focus on:

- Character relationships
- Emotional / desire dynamics
- Inciting events
- Conflicts between characters
- Story development

Erotic scenes should be described using:

> **Neutral, objective, plot-oriented language.**

Do not turn the synopsis into a description of sexual content.

---

## Crime Films

Focus on:

- The crime
- Criminals
- Victims
- Investigation
- Character relationships
- Escalation of the conflict

The reader should understand:

> **What happened, what the central case is, and who became involved.**

---

## Horror Films

Focus on:

- Who the protagonist is
- Where they go
- What strange or supernatural events occur
- How the danger escalates
- How the characters become involved

Do not simply write:

> “The film creates a dark and terrifying atmosphere.”

---

## Romance Films

Focus on:

- Main relationship
- How the characters meet
- How the relationship develops
- Obstacles
- Changes in the relationship

---

## Action Films

Focus on:

- Protagonist
- Mission / event
- Opponent
- Conflict
- Action and plot development

---

## Mystery / Suspense Films

Focus on:

- What the mystery or case is
- Who is investigating
- Known clues
- Main character relationships

Avoid directly revealing the final answer.

---

## Fantasy / Supernatural / Folklore Films

Focus on:

- World / historical setting
- Character identities
- Supernatural elements
- Character relationships
- Central conflict

Do not simply write:

> “The film combines Eastern fantasy elements with an eerie atmosphere.”

Explain what actually happens.

---

# 18. Final Synopsis Quality Checklist

Every synopsis must be checked for:

## Plot

- [ ] Is the protagonist clearly identified?
- [ ] Is the starting situation explained?
- [ ] Is the inciting event explained?
- [ ] Is the central conflict clear?
- [ ] Is the main plot development explained?
- [ ] Can the reader answer “What is this movie about?” after reading it?

## Accuracy

- [ ] Are the character relationships correct?
- [ ] Does the plot match the actual film?
- [ ] Is anything invented?
- [ ] Has a similarly titled film been confused with this one?
- [ ] Has the plot of another adaptation or version been accidentally mixed in?

## Writing

- [ ] Is the synopsis primarily plot-focused?
- [ ] Has vague commentary been minimized?
- [ ] Is there no promotional language?
- [ ] Is excessive thematic analysis avoided?
- [ ] Is the ending not unnecessarily revealed?
- [ ] Is the length reasonable?

If the reader finishes the synopsis and thinks:

> **“I still don't know what this movie is about.”**

the synopsis is considered unacceptable and must be rewritten.

# 19. Film Classification

Use the following 15 primary categories:

1. Drama
2. Action
3. Science Fiction
4. Comedy
5. Romance
6. Mystery / Suspense
7. Horror
8. Animation
9. Documentary
10. Erotic
11. Crime
12. Fantasy
13. History
14. Wuxia / Martial Arts
15. Other

Each film should receive one primary category.

Erotic / Category III films generally use:

> **Erotic**

If the crime or criminal-case element is clearly more important than the erotic component:

> **Crime**

If horror is clearly the central narrative and genre identity:

> **Horror**

Classification should be based on the film's primary narrative identity rather than simply whether a particular element appears in the film.

# 20. Tag System

Tags are used for filtering and organization.

They must not be used as a substitute for the synopsis.

Possible tag groups include:

### Themes

Human nature, desire, revenge, romance, family, society, power, etc.

### Region

Hong Kong, Taiwan, Mainland China, Japan, South Korea, Europe, North America, etc.

### Era

Ancient, Republican Era, modern, contemporary, etc.

### Series

Specific franchise or series name.

### Elements

Police, investigation, ghosts, monsters, martial arts, prison, war, etc.

### Mood / Atmosphere

Oppressive, dark, eerie, absurd, tragic, etc.

### Form

Anthology, sequel, adaptation, based on a true story, etc.

### Highlights

Cult, genre landmark, ensemble cast, low-budget, Category III, etc.

Use no more than 6 values in a single tag field.

# 22. Standard Production Workflow

When processing a batch of films, follow this workflow:

### Step 1: Identify the Film

Confirm:

- Official title
- Release year
- Whether multiple films or versions share similar titles

### Step 2: Research the Film

Verify:

- Director
- Cast
- Main characters
- Story setting
- Plot
- Genre

### Step 3: Determine the Primary Category

Select the single category that best represents the film's core identity from the 15 categories.

### Step 4: Perform the Ten-Dimension Rating

Evaluate:

> Screenplay → Direction → Acting → Cinematography & Production Design → Editing & Pacing → Music & Sound → Themes → Originality → Production Quality → Series / Genre Significance

### Step 5: Calculate the Final Rating

Apply the specified weights and round to two decimal places.

### Step 6: Write the Synopsis

Follow:

> **Character → Inciting Event → Conflict → Development → Characteristics**

Plot comes first.

### Step 7: Perform the Synopsis Quality Check

Ask:

> **“Can someone who has never seen this film understand what actually happens in it after reading this synopsis?”**

If not, rewrite it.

### Step 8: Generate the Excel File

Keep consistent:

- Formatting
- Fields
- Synopsis length
- Decimal places
- Tags
- Ordering

# 23. The Three Most Important Principles of v4.0

Regardless of the film being processed, always prioritize these three principles:

### Principle 1: Rate Fairly

> **Do not discriminate against a film because of its genre, and do not inflate it because of its reputation. Judge its actual execution within its genre and production context.**

### Principle 2: Make the Synopsis Tell the Story

> **Do not merely tell me what kind of film it is. First tell me what happens in the film.**

### Principle 3: Research Before Writing

> **If the information is uncertain, research it. If it cannot be verified, do not invent it.**

The entire standard can ultimately be summarized as:

> **“Rate the execution, explain the story, classify by core identity, and verify the facts before writing.”**
